NHS HealthStore · Site Information Architecture 01 · Scope & approach

01 · Scope & approach

What this document is

An as-is record of the NHS HealthStore alpha's information architecture, captured on 28 July 2026. It documents every page a user can actually reach through the live navigation and on-page links, the global navigation furniture, and the journeys those pages support.

The service in one paragraph

HealthStore is a public, open-access storefront for NHS commissioners. It lets them browse an assured catalogue of digital therapeutics by care pathway, review detailed product profiles (evidence, commercial and implementation information), compare up to four products side by side, scan funding opportunities, read supporting guidance and case studies, and express interest in a product via a structured multi-step form. There is no purchase or checkout — “express interest” is the commercial front door.

Method

  • Walked every navigation entry point and followed all on-page links from the live build.
  • Verified each page renders and is reachable without sign-in (the alpha runs in open access — no account is required and no sign-in is offered).
  • Cross-checked against the codebase to confirm nothing reachable was missed.

Explicitly out of scope

Functionality that exists in the codebase but is currently hidden, disabled or unreachable is not documented here. This includes anything behind feature flags or auth gates, legacy URL redirects, and pages with no inbound links. If any of these are switched on, this document should be re-issued.

Conventions used

Throughout the document:

  • Content  primary content page
  • Action  page containing the journey's key call to action or form
  • Utility  policy / housekeeping page
  • /apps/[slug] — square brackets denote a dynamic URL segment (one page template, many products)

Counts at a glance

  • 15 distinct visible pages (13 static + 2 dynamic templates)
  • 5 primary navigation items + logo home link
  • 1 conversion journey (express interest)
  • 0 pages requiring sign-in

Persistent furniture

  • Phase banner (Alpha) — sits directly beneath the header on every page, signalling service maturity.
  • Footer — a single link: Cookies. Deliberately minimal for the alpha.
  • Cookie consent banner — first-visit overlay for analytics consent; consent can be reset from the Cookies page.

Design observation

The whole service is open access: there is no sign-in, no account area, and therefore no logged-in/logged-out navigation states to design for in the current alpha.

05 · Primary user flow — discover, assess, express interest

The core service journey for a commissioner. There is no basket or checkout — the expression of interest is the commercial hand-off, after which the conversation moves off-platform. Every step is reachable without an account.

Happy path
1 · Arrive/Lands on home; understands the proposition; picks a care pathway chip or heads to the catalogue.
→
2 · Browse/catalogueChooses a condition / pathway from the hub cards.
→
3 · Filter/catalogue/digital-therapeuticsSearches and filters the assured list; scans product cards.
→
4 · Assess/apps/[slug]Reviews the product profile: evidence, commercial terms, implementation and NHS experience.
→
5 · Act/apps/[slug]/express-interestCompletes the multi-step expression-of-interest form and submits.
→
6 · Confirmed(confirmation step)Sees confirmation; signposted to guidance or back to the product page.
Common detours from step 4 (product detail page)
Assess/apps/[slug]Not ready to commit to one product.
→
Add to compare/compareShortlists up to 4 products; compares side by side; returns to the strongest candidate's page.
→
Back to assess/apps/[slug]Continues to express interest.
Assess/apps/[slug]Needs to understand affordability first.
→
Check funding/fundingReviews open and upcoming funding routes; follows external links for detail.
→
Back to assess/apps/[slug]Returns via the related product links.

Service design note

The journey is fully self-serve up to submission. The moment of hand-off (step 6) is the boundary of the digital service — follow-up currently happens outside HealthStore, which is worth making explicit in stakeholder conversations about scope.

Pattern across all three flows

Every supporting flow is designed to feed back into the primary flow at the product detail page or catalogue. The IA has a single centre of gravity — the product profile — which keeps the model simple to explain and test.

07 · Product detail page anatomy — the service's key template (/apps/[slug]), as live on myCOPD and Luscii

Breadcrumb: Product catalogue → Digital therapeutics → {product}

Platform · Indicative cost · Technical integration · Live NHSE sites — each tile deep-links into the page

“On this page”

(links to sections 4–13)
4The problem {product} addresses
5How {product} changes a treatment pathway — current vs with-product comparison

8Funding levers and tariff alignment

13Resources — demo access; product videos where available
  1. 1Hero & proposition (1–2). What the product is, its assured status, and the two decisions we ask of the user — commit (express interest) or shortlist (compare) — before the long-form content begins.
  2. 3Commissioning snapshot. Four scannable tiles for time-poor commissioners; each deep-links to the relevant section (cost → how to commission, sites → NHS experience, demo → resources).
  3. A“On this page” navigation. Sticky side navigation listing all ten narrative sections, so users can jump straight to funding or assurance — the two most-asked-about topics.
  4. 4Narrative spine (4–13). Sequenced to answer a commissioner's questions in the order they arise: what problem is this solving (4–5), what would it mean here (6–7), how is it paid for (8), is it safe (9), what does it take (10), who else has done it (11), how do I buy it (12), and where do I dig deeper (13).
  5. 12Second conversion point. The express-interest callout repeats at the end of “How to commission and procure”, catching users who arrive at commitment only after reading the full case.

myCOPD vs Luscii — same template, content-level differences

Both products render this exact structure. Differences are in content, not anatomy: myCOPD additionally shows a linked funding scheme (under 8), onboarding / ongoing-support steps (under 10) and product videos (under 13); Luscii additionally shows a technical integration tile (FHIR · EMIS) in the snapshot. Impact charts (6) are modelled per product. There are no detail tabs on either page.
